# data-science-job-market-analysis

Analysis of Data Science job market and salary trends using Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, and Seaborn

# Analysis on Data Science Job Market and Salary

## Project Summary

This project looks at job market and salary patterns across data-related occupations, where I use Python to conduct the analysis. The study examines how salaries differ across occupations, experience levels, work models and time periods while also analyzing the most frequently represented data-related roles within the dataset.

# Main areas of Focus
- Which data-related occupations are the most common in the dataset?
- Which job titles earn the highest average salaries?
- How do salaries vary across remote, hybrid, and on-site positions?
- How have salaries for data-related occupations changed over time?

## Tools 
- Python
- Pandas
- NumPy
- Matplotlib
- Seaborn
- Google Colab

## Dataset
The selected dataset consists of 3,755 observations of data-related positions from 2020-2023. It contains the following factors below:

- Job title
- Experience Level
- Employment type
- Salary
- Employee residence
- Remote work ratio
- Company location
- Company size

Comparisons regarding salaries use 'salary_in_usd' in the analysis to present a consistent measure across all observations.

## Analysis

The project contains the following:

- Exploratory data analysis (EDA) and data quality evaluation
- Categorical data preparation and cleaning
- Job title distribution
- Salary comparison by job title
- Salary comparison by experience level
- Salary comparison by work model
- Salary patterns over time
- Data visualization and insights

# Notable Insights
- Data Engineer was the most commonly observed job title in the selected dataset, where Data Scientist came in second and Data Analyst followed in third regarding common job titles.
- Data Science Manager and Applied Scientist had the highest average annual salaries among job titles with at least 20 observations in the dataset.
- Higher annual salaries were associated with higher experience levels as presented in the dataset.
- In comparing all three work models, on-site employees earned the highest average salaries compared to those who worked remote or hybrid positions. Remote occupations earned a little less than on-site positions, whereas hybrid workers earned significantly less than both on-site and remote occupations.
- Over time, average salaries increased from 2020-2023, yet the number of observations differed significantly by year.

These results showcase trends within the dataset and should not be evaluated as representing the overall market for data-related positions.
